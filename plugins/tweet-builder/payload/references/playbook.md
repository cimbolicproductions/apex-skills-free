## Character counts (hard limits & sweet spots)

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Standard tweet max | **280 characters** |
| Single-tweet sweet spot | **71–100 characters** (most retweets; ~17% higher engagement under ~100) |
| Bio max | 160 characters |
| Hashtags | **1–2 max**, ~6 chars or fewer; the Grok model reads content directly so hashtags are mostly irrelevant — overuse (5+) is penalized |
| Thread hook | full 280 available — but same hook principles apply |
| Thread length | **7–10 tweets** is the proven range |
| Premium long-post | up to 25,000 chars (Premium only) |

Rule of thumb: **one idea per tweet, concise, no fluff.** Don't max out 280 by default.

Source: charactercounter.com; PRNEWS (TrackSocial + Buddy Media studies).

## The 2026 algorithm — what it rewards

X now uses a Grok-powered transformer that reads posts and watches videos; ~5B ranking decisions/day. It rewards **conversation over vanity metrics.**

Engagement weights (vs. Like = 1x):

| Action | Weight |
|---|---|
| Like | 1x |
| Bookmark | ~10x |
| Retweet/Repost | ~20x |
| Quote tweet | ~25x |
| Reply | ~27x |
| **Reply that the author replies to** | **~150x** |
| Profile click | ~12x |
| Dwell time | high, variable |

(Numbers are estimates from the open-sourced ranking model + 2026 observation; ranking order is consistent across sources, exact figures are not.)

Mechanics:
- **First 30–60 min are critical** — early velocity decides reach.
- **Time decay:** ~half the visibility score lost every 6 hours; minimal push after 24h.
- **Test audience:** post shown to ~5–15% of followers first, then expands if it clears threshold.
- **Feed split:** ~50% follows / ~50% non-follows in "For You."
- **Posting cadence:** 2–5/day is the growth sweet spot; 1/day minimum; 10+ = spam.
- **Best windows:** weekday 8–10 AM & 12–1 PM (audience local); **Wednesday 9 AM strongest**; weekends weakest.

Source: OpenTweet 2026; Sprout Social; Tweet Archivist.

## Hook formulas (the most important sentence)

A hook has two jobs: **(1) stop the scroll, (2) earn the next line.** Every word serves one of those or gets cut.

### 5 elements of an effective headline
1. **Clear, not clever** — plain promise.
2. **Specify the WHO** — name the audience so they self-select.
3. **Specify the WHAT** — exactly what they get.
4. **Specify the WHY** — the payoff.
5. **Twist the knife** — agitate the pain.

### 6 ways to open the first sentence (the "angle menu")
1. Strong declarative sentence (bold/controversial claim)
2. Thought-provoking question
3. Controversial opinion ("silent" opinion everyone thinks but won't say)
4. Moment in time ("When Iron Man came out in 2008…")
5. Vulnerable statement (personal admission)
6. Weird, unique insight (surprising fact)

Hook boosters: curiosity gap, credibility, specificity (numbers/names/timeframes), a scroll-stopping image.

Source: Ship30for30.

## The 6 questions behind every great post
1. What problem am I solving?
2. Whose problem?
3. What are the benefits of solving it?
4. What promise am I making?
5. What emotion am I generating?
6. What's the next action for the reader?

(These map directly to the Socratic loop in SKILL.md.) Source: Ship30for30.

## Thread architecture
- ~3x total engagement of an equivalent single tweet (dwell time + replies).
- **Tweet 1:** full hook + optional image; signal it's a thread (":" or 🧵).
- **Tweet 2:** as important as the hook — give a reason to keep reading; open a loop.
- **Slippery slope:** each line pulls to the next; end middle tweets on mini-cliffhangers.
- **Format:** short sentences, white space, headers, numbering (2/12, 3/12…).
- **Story:** include a personal experience to drive follows.
- **Close:** summary recap + CTA in priority order (RT first tweet → follow → bookmark).
- **Loop back:** reply to the last tweet with a link to the top tweet, ask for RT.

Source: Ship30for30; Legiit.

## Media & links
| Format | Effect |
|---|---|
| Native video | strongest (~10x text-only); even a 30-sec clip; authenticity > polish |
| Image / GIF | moderate boost; avoid heavy text on image |
| Poll | drives replies |
| Text-only | neutral |
| **External link in main tweet** | **severe suppression** — put links in the **first reply** instead |

Source: OpenTweet; X Business.

## Linguistic patterns of retweetable tweets
- Newsy, headline-style **subject–verb–object**; heavy on nouns/proper nouns/third-person verbs.
- Simple sentences; mostly 1–2 syllable words.
- **Use punctuation** (98% of retweets have it vs. 86% of normal tweets).
- Skip lazy slang (lol, gonna, hey).

Source: PRNEWS (Zarrella).

## Do / Don't

**Do:** lead with value; end with a reply-prompting question/opinion; reply to every reply in first 15 min; use native video; ~71–100 chars/single tweet, one idea; conversational tone, sparing emojis; clear CTA; post 2–5x/day at peak; warm up by replying to 5–10 niche posts before publishing; stay consistent.

**Don't:** put external links in the main tweet; ALL CAPS; stuff hashtags; obvious engagement bait ("Like if you agree"); 10 tweets in 5 min; delete-and-repost identical content; max 280 with fluff; trigger reports/blocks/"not interested."

---

## Pre-publish checklist
- [ ] Hook stops the scroll AND earns the next line
- [ ] Exact WHO named
- [ ] WHAT + WHY (payoff) explicit
- [ ] Invites a reply (question / opinion / curiosity gap)
- [ ] One idea per tweet; single tweets ~71–100 chars
- [ ] Simple words, real punctuation, no lazy slang
- [ ] No external link in main tweet (→ first reply)
- [ ] ≤1–2 hashtags, no ALL CAPS, no engagement bait
- [ ] Media attached if it raises the ceiling
- [ ] Posting in a peak window
- [ ] 15 min free after to reply to replies
